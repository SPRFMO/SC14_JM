Which management rules may work best?

How can we choose rules that support catches while performing acceptably when the future is uncertain?

Management strategy evaluation (MSE) tests management procedures in simulated fisheries. A procedure specifies how information is used to make a decision, such as a catch recommendation. The simulation repeats the process over time so that the consequences of earlier decisions affect later ones.

What the research has examined

The working-group papers compare eight candidate management procedures. A complementary study presents another evaluation with different assumptions and methods. The workshop report, main studies and independent review provide the evidence for SC14’s discussion. Sources: JM03, JM04, JM06, JM07, JM12.

Why it matters for management

There is more to consider than average catch. Managers also need to understand biological performance, changes in catch from year to year, and how a rule behaves in plausible difficult conditions. A rule tuned to one target still requires evaluation against the full set of relevant criteria.

Comparing the two evaluations’ rankings requires comparable assumptions and performance measures. This guide highlights that interpretation question for readers; the Committee’s treatment belongs in its meeting record. Sources: JM06, JM07, JM12.

What has been agreed?

The papers and workshop recommendations are available for review. Any Scientific Committee recommendation or Commission adoption will require its respective authoritative decision record.

What remains to be discussed?

Which findings are sufficiently supported to inform advice? Which candidates merit further consideration? What must be completed before a procedure can be used each year?

That last question includes clear instructions for the calculation and for unusual circumstances: what to monitor, what triggers concern, who considers it and what happens next. Sources: JM06, JM08, JM09.
